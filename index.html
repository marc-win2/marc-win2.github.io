Marc Winstel

CBDC · digital money · financial-market infrastructure · scientific computing

Researcher at Deutsche Bundesbank

Frankfurt am Main, Germany

I am a researcher working at the intersection of central bank digital currency (CBDC), distributed ledger technology, and financial-market infrastructure. At Deutsche Bundesbank, my research focuses on the technical and institutional design of CBDC for retail and wholesale purposes, including but not limited to consensus mechanisms for settlement, offline payments, and DLT architectures for financial markets. For details, please feel free to reach out or check out my CV.

My route into this work began in theoretical and computational physics. During my doctorate at Goethe University Frankfurt, which I completed summa cum laude, I developed analytical and numerical approaches to strongly interacting matter and built scientific software in C++ and Python. This research led to eight peer-reviewed journal publications, including a single-author paper and a paper in Physical Review Letters.

Across both fields, my work centers on the same task: turning technically complex questions into structured, testable analysis and communicating the result to specialist and non-specialist audiences. Alongside research, I have coordinated seminars and teaching teams, contributed to research-center review processes, given more than 25 conference and seminar presentations, and taught theoretical and computational physics across more than ten semesters.

Outside work, I am a passionate horse rider and showjumper, and I am generally interested in (watching and doing) sports. I also started playing a little bit of chess.

You can find my research on the publications page, look into a brief CV, or read more about my teaching.
